# Information hierarchy

Recipe discovery uses aligned photo cards with one, two, or three columns based on available width. Each card shows the meal name, preparation time, servings and pickup estimate. Cuisine chips filter the catalog and guide AI suggestions.

| Level | Purpose | Visible information |
| --- | --- | --- |
| L1 — Decide | Take the next step | Meal request or selected meal, today’s payable total, current people/budget/time summary, primary action, active exclusions and blocking warnings |
| L2 — Adjust | Change or check the decision | Preferences, mood, shopping ingredients, owned ingredients, changes to an AI meal, weekly cart quantities, pantry editing |
| L3 — Explain | Understand supporting details | Price components, agent comparison trace, estimate assumptions, privacy and demo details |

Initial AI view leads with the meal request. A suggested meal leads with its name, constraints, price and Review basket action. Clarification questions remain visible when no plan exists. Active ingredient exclusions, certification-review notices, unavailable baskets and over-budget states remain visible.

Expandable sections have descriptive labels, current-value summaries, a plus/minus affordance and accessibility expansion state. Price breakdowns open on demand; future rewards remain separate from today’s charge. Weekly automation and local fallback planning sit under Planning options, with their on/off state summarized outside. Long grocery lists open on demand.

Four visible pastel navigation tiles replace the horizontal scroller. Wrap-up navigation has been removed; saved nutrition data is preserved. A consistent Meal → Basket → Store → Checkout indicator distinguishes completed, current and upcoming steps. Preference sliders group their labels, current values and endpoints in tinted panels.

Pattern reference: Nielsen Norman Group, Progressive Disclosure: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/progressive-disclosure/
